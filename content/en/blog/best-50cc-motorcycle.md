## Criteria for choosing a 50cc motorcycle

**License requirements** are the first filter. The AM license (obtained after the BSR in France, similar learner permits across Europe) lets riders aged 14 ride a 50cc capped at 28 mph (45 km/h). No car license is needed. In the US, most states allow 50cc motorcycles on a regular driver's license or a basic moped permit at age 14-16 depending on the state.

**Engine type** splits the market. **Two-stroke** (Beta RR, Derbi Senda, Sherco SE) delivers more punch on hills, lighter weight, but requires premix oil and more frequent maintenance. **Four-stroke** (Mash Fifty, Hanway, Rieju MRT 4T) is more fuel-efficient, more durable, but less peppy.

**Use segment** drives the model choice. **Supermoto** (Beta RR Racing, Derbi Senda DRD) is fast and fun in the city, with 17-inch wheels and street tires. **Enduro** (Sherco SE, Rieju MRT) tackles trails while staying road-friendly. **50cc neo-retro** (Mash Fifty, Brixton 50) plays the style card at a tight price.

**The 28 mph and 6 kW cap** applies to every road-legal 50cc in Europe. Any derestricted bike becomes illegal (insurance void, possible impoundment). Pure racing versions (track only) are not road-legal.

## 5-year ownership cost

Purchase price varies by 2x across the segment. $2,690 for the Mash Fifty, $4,090 for the Fantic, $4,790 for the Beta RR Racing, $5,090 for the Sherco premium.

**Insurance** for a 50cc motorcycle stays modest: $250-500 per year for a learner rider, $150-300 for an experienced rider. Liability-only coverage is enough for an entry-level 50cc bike.

**Maintenance** depends on the engine. A 2-stroke needs premix oil (2% in fuel or separate tank), spark plug every 3,000 miles, piston rings and base gasket every 5,000-6,000 miles. A 4-stroke holds 7,500 miles between oil changes, simpler to manage. Over 5 years at 2,500 miles per year, count on $800-1,400 USD in total maintenance.

**Fuel** runs 60-90 mpg, which is $100-150 per year for 2,500 miles. The 50cc motorcycle remains the cheapest motorized daily commuter after the bicycle.

## License and regulations

The **AM license** is required to ride a 50cc at age 14 in France. It's earned via theory training (ASSR in school or ASR for non-students) followed by 8 hours of practical training (around $250-350 at riding schools). Riders born before January 1, 1988 can use the regular B car license without further training. In the US, most states allow 50cc motorcycle use on a regular driver's license or learner's permit, with rules varying by state.

The **legal cap** is 28 mph (45 km/h) and 6 kW. Derestricting is illegal, voids insurance, and risks impoundment plus a fine. Pure Racing competition versions (no plate) are exempt.

## FAQ best 50cc motorcycle

### Which 50cc motorcycle is best for beginners?

The **Mash Fifty** ($2,690) is the most accessible on budget and riding ease, perfect for starting at 14. For a step up in performance and reliability, the **Beta RR 50 Track** ($4,690) remains the versatile sport benchmark, provided you're OK managing a 2-stroke.

### Which 50cc motorcycle uses the least fuel?

The **Fantic Caballero 50** and **Mash Fifty** (both 4-stroke) average around 90 mpg in urban use. Two-stroke models (Beta, Derbi, Sherco) consume rather 60-75 mpg, plus premix oil cost (around $40-60 per year extra).

### Should I pick a 2-stroke or 4-stroke 50cc?

**Two-stroke** delivers more torque, lighter weight, sharper acceleration, but demands attention (premix, gaskets to swap). **Four-stroke** is simpler, more durable, more fuel-efficient, but less lively. For an urban beginner, 4-stroke is more relaxed. For riders who love piloting and high-rev riding, 2-stroke remains king of the 50cc.
