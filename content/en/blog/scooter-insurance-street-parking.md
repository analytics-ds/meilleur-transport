> **At a glance**
> - The main risk to an urban scooter is not a crash, it is **what happens while it is parked**. Theft, a failed theft attempt that wrecks the ignition, a broken mirror, the bike laid flat on the pavement by a driver who leaves no note.
> - **None of that is covered by third-party insurance**, and the most frequent of them, a scooter knocked over by an unidentified party, is not covered by third-party fire and theft either. That needs accidental damage cover.
> - The costliest trap is not the policy level but **the lock clause**. A non-approved lock, or simply one that was not in use on the night, and the claim fails whatever the premium paid.

A scooter that sleeps on the street does not face the same risks as a motorcycle taken out at weekends. The cover calculation changes completely, and most policies are chosen on headline price without checking what happens while the vehicle is stationary. This guide takes each situation and the cover that answers it. It complements our [comparison of scooter insurers](/en/blog/best-scooter-insurers/).

## Four risks to a scooter parked outside, and the cover that answers {#table}

| Situation | Urban frequency | Third party | Fire and theft | Fully comprehensive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scooter stolen | very high | no | yes | yes |
| Attempted theft damaging ignition or bodywork | high | no | yes | yes |
| Scooter knocked over or scratched, party unknown | very high | no | **no** | yes |
| Fire, including from a neighbouring vehicle | moderate | no | yes | yes |
| Damage caused to a third party by the falling scooter | low | yes | yes | yes |

The third row is the one to remember. It is the most ordinary event in city life, a scooter lying on the pavement with nobody to own up, and it is the one mid-range policies do not cover. Many riders discover the exclusion on the day of the loss, having assumed fire and theft protected them against everything that happens at a standstill.

## What city parking actually changes {#city}

Three differences compared with a bike kept in a garage.

Exposure time first. A commuter scooter spends most of its life stationary on a public road, often in the same spot at the same hours. That regularity is exactly what makes organised theft possible, and it is why insurers rate the parking location so heavily.

Anonymity next. In a city, an impact at a standstill almost always leaves no identified party. No accident report, no third party, therefore no recourse and no payout outside accidental damage cover.

Density last. A fire from a neighbouring vehicle, a domino fall along a tightly packed row of bikes, a street works operation: these are losses with no direct culprit that only a complete policy absorbs.

## What third-party cover never includes {#third-party}

Third-party liability is the legal minimum and stays compulsory, including for a vehicle that never moves. It covers what the scooter inflicts on others, never what it suffers itself.

In practice, with third-party cover: a stolen scooter is not reimbursed, forced bodywork is not repaired, a torn-off mirror stays at your expense, and so does the bike laid flat on the pavement. The only thing handled is damage caused to a third party, for instance the car door scratched by the scooter as it falls.

That is a legitimate choice on an old, low-value scooter, provided it is made knowingly. The useful test is always the same: whether you could replace the vehicle overnight without it being a problem. We set out that trade-off in our guide to [third-party versus comprehensive motorcycle insurance](/en/blog/third-party-or-comprehensive-motorcycle-insurance/).

## The covers that matter when the scooter sleeps outside {#covers}

### Theft and attempted theft {#theft}

The baseline for urban use. It covers the vehicle disappearing but also, and this gets overlooked, the damage from a failed attempt: forced ignition, ripped-out switch, cracked bodywork. Those repairs often run into several hundred euros even on a low-value scooter, which makes the cover worthwhile on a modest bike.

### Fire {#fire}

Almost always sold alongside theft, it earns its place in dense parking, where fire rarely starts on the insured vehicle itself but on its immediate neighbour.

### Accidental damage {#accidental}

The cover that changes everything in a city, and the one most often missing from policies chosen on price. It pays for damage suffered by the scooter whatever the cause, with or without a collision, with or without an identified third party, less the policy excess where the rider is at fault. It is the only answer to a scooter knocked over in a car park.

### Rider equipment {#equipment}

The helmet very often stays strapped to the bike, sometimes the gloves with it. They therefore leave with it in a theft. This cover is a good marker of how serious a policy is: some insurers reserve it for upper tiers, others include it from the start. At **AMV** it applies from the entry-level policy, up to 250 euros for the helmet, 70 euros for gloves and 500 euros for an airbag vest, less wear and tear. Our guide to [motorcycle equipment cover](/en/blog/motorcycle-gear-insurance/) goes into detail.

### Personal injury cover {#rider}

It has nothing to do with parking but deserves a look when choosing, because it is the only cover that protects the rider in an at-fault accident. At AMV, the Individuelle Pilote option provides a lump sum of up to 800 000 euros in the event of permanent disability, or to the beneficiaries in the event of death.

## The lock clause, what voids most theft claims {#lock}

This is the leading cause of rejection on a theft claim, well ahead of arguments about value.

Most policies make theft cover conditional on an approved lock, and at claim stage ask for the purchase invoice, sometimes the key, sometimes both. Three situations void the payout: a non-approved lock, an approved lock that was not in use at the time, and a lock whose invoice has been lost.

Two habits are enough to secure the file. Keep the lock invoice with the vehicle documents, photographed on your phone rather than filed in a drawer. And check, before signing, whether the policy requires the lock to be attached to a fixed anchor point or merely fitted to the wheel, because the day-to-day difference is real.

Some insurers grant a premium discount in return for an approved lock, often around 5 to 10 %, which offsets part of the hardware cost.

## Matching the policy to the parking spot {#location}

Parking location is among the heaviest rating factors, alongside the model, declared claims history, level of cover and licence seniority.

A scooter kept in a locked box does not carry the risk profile of one spending the night on a city pavement. Between the two, a gated courtyard is a middle ground worth declaring, because many riders declare it as public road out of convenience and pay more than they need to.

The practical point: this factor can be updated mid-policy. A house move, access to a workplace car park or renting a covered space are all legitimate grounds for revising a premium, provided the insurer is told.

## Which insurer for a scooter parked in a city {#insurer}

On this profile, the value of a two-wheeler specialist lies in how finely the range is graded. A generalist often offers two levels, which forces you to buy the full comprehensive policy just to obtain accidental damage cover.

**AMV** splits its offer into four progressive levels, from third-party liability through fire and theft and collision damage to accidental damage. That gradation makes it possible to match cover to the scooter's real value rather than jumping from one extreme to the other. The insurer has more than 1 million policyholders and holds a 4.7/5 rating on Avis Vérifiés. Its Assistance 0 km option applies with no minimum distance, 24/7, including after theft, attempted theft or removal by the pound, which maps precisely onto the misfortunes of an urban two-wheeler.

La Mutuelle des Motards and April Moto take comparable approaches on two-wheelers. Since pricing and cover details move regularly, it is better to check at the point of purchase than to rely on a fixed ranking. One final criterion, more useful than the headline price: how clearly the theft claim conditions are set out. A policy that states in black and white which lock is required, which documents are needed and what waiting period applies saves you the nasty surprise on claim day. One that stays vague on those three points is selling a promise you cannot check until it is too late.
