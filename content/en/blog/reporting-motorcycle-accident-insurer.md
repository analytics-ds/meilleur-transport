## In brief

1. The deadline is **five working days**, not five days. The distinction shifts the expiry by two days for an accident at the end of the week.
2. The e-constat app does cover motorcycles, but **it is unusable as soon as anyone is injured**. On a motorcycle, that is the most common outcome.
3. A late report **does not automatically mean refusal**: the insurer has to prove the delay caused it prejudice.

After a fall or a collision, the question is rarely whether to report, but what to send and within what deadline. The answers sit in a single article of the French Insurance Code and in the terms of use of the insurers' own app, two short texts almost nobody reads before needing them. This guide extracts the exact list of items to gather, the deadlines that are actually enforceable, and the cases where the digital tool will not step in.

## Reporting deadlines, claim by claim {#table}

| Type of claim | Legal minimum deadline | Start of the period |
|---|---|---|
| Accident, with or without a third party | **5 working days** | The day the loss becomes known |
| Solo fall, no third party | **5 working days** | The day the loss becomes known |
| Broken glass, fire | **5 working days** | The day the loss becomes known |
| Theft, attempted theft, vandalism | **2 working days** | The day the facts are discovered |
| Natural disaster | Per the government order | Publication of the order in the Journal officiel |

These periods are **legal minimums** set by Article L113-2 of the French Insurance Code, which states that the contractual deadline "cannot be shorter than five working days" and that it "is reduced to two working days in the event of theft". A policy may therefore extend them, never shorten them.

Two traps hide in that reading. The first is the word **working**, routinely dropped: Sundays and public holidays do not count, so a Friday evening accident leaves until the following Friday rather than the Wednesday. The second is the display of deadlines **in hours**. A phrase such as "within 48 hours" for a theft is shorter than the two working days the law guarantees, since a theft discovered on a Friday evening would then expire on the Sunday instead of the Tuesday.

The starting point is worth noting too: the text refers to the moment the policyholder **became aware** of the loss, not the date of the accident. The distinction matters for a parked two-wheeler damaged without its owner knowing.

## What actually has to be sent {#content}

A claim report is a set of dated facts, not a narrative. Six blocks make it up.

- **The circumstances**, with the date, time and exact location. Even a rough sketch beats a paragraph.
- **The third party's identity** where there is one: name, registration plate, insurance company and policy number, taken from the insurance certificate.
- **Material damage**, to the machine but also to the rider's gear. Helmet, jacket, gloves and boots form a category of their own, often covered by a dedicated option.
- **Bodily injury**, including minor injuries. This is the item most frequently omitted and the most expensive to omit: after-effects appearing later will only be covered if the claim was reported as involving bodily injury.
- **Witnesses**, with their contact details. They become decisive as soon as the two accounts diverge.
- **Attachments**: the accident statement, photographs taken at the scene, and the police report where one was drawn up.

On photographs, one simple reflex changes the value of the file: frame the vehicles **in their environment** before moving them, including road signs, lane markings and the state of the surface. Once the machines are on the verge, the scene can no longer be reconstructed.

The statement form itself follows a little-known rule: only the **front side** establishes liability. The reverse merely details the circumstances. A disagreement therefore belongs in the observations box on the front, not on the back.

## The e-constat app on a motorcycle: what it covers, and the three cases where it fails {#econstat}

The e-constat auto is the official app of the French insurance industry, published under the aegis of France Assureurs. It **covers motorised two-wheelers and three-wheelers**, on the same footing as cars, bicycles and motorised personal mobility devices, provided they are registered and insured in France.

| Situation | e-constat app | Paper form |
|---|---|---|
| Two vehicles, material damage only | Yes | Yes |
| A single vehicle involved | Yes | Yes |
| Motorised two-wheeler registered in France | Yes | Yes |
| **Any bodily injury at all** | **No** | Yes |
| More than two vehicles involved | **No** | Yes |
| Foreign vehicle, or accident outside France | **No** | Yes |

The first exclusion is the one that concerns riders most directly. A motorcycle collision rarely ends in nothing more than dented bodywork, and **the slightest injury takes the case outside the app's scope**. The tool is well suited to car drivers and regularly inoperative for riders, on precisely the day they would need it.

The practical consequence fits in one line: keep a paper form under the seat or in the top box. It takes up no space and remains the only valid document in the three cases above. That precaution sits alongside the [essential motorcycle insurance covers](/en/blog/essential-motorcycle-insurance-cover/), which are likewise checked before a claim rather than after.

## A late report is not a conviction {#late}

The belief that a single day of delay forfeits the payout is widespread and inaccurate. The final paragraph of Article L113-2 frames the sanction strictly: forfeiture for late reporting "can only be invoked against the policyholder if the insurer establishes that the delay caused it prejudice". The burden of proof therefore lies with the insurer, and it bears on actual prejudice, not on the principle of lateness.

The same text adds that forfeiture can never be invoked "in all cases where the delay is due to a fortuitous event or force majeure". A rider hospitalised after a crash falls squarely within that category.

Three reflexes follow. Report as soon as health allows, without waiting to have gathered every document, since a report can be completed later. State the reason for the delay explicitly, hospitalisation or immobilisation, so that it is on record. Keep proof of the date of sending, an acknowledgement of receipt or a timestamp from the online account.

## Disputed liability, hit-and-run, uninsured third party {#disputes}

Three situations fall outside the ordinary accident statement and are worth anticipating.

**Disagreement over the circumstances.** Nothing requires signing a statement believed to be inaccurate. Reservations go in the observations box, and the insurer then decides on the basis of photographs, witness statements and findings. Legal protection cover takes on the dispute, up to a ceiling that is better known before it is needed.

**Hit-and-run.** Note the registration, model, colour and direction taken, look for witnesses and identify cameras covering the location. The statement is filled in regardless, describing the facts, and the incident is reported to the police immediately.

**Uninsured or unidentified third party.** The French guarantee fund for compulsory damage insurance may compensate the victim, subject to conditions. Filing a police report and notifying the insurer remain prerequisites, including where the policy does not cover the situation, failing which no recourse can be pursued. This is also the scenario where comprehensive cover changes everything, an arbitration set out in the comparison of [third-party or comprehensive motorcycle insurance](/en/blog/third-party-or-comprehensive-motorcycle-insurance/).

## What reporting changes, and what it does not {#consequences}

One worry comes up every time: the effect on the premium. It deserves a precise answer.

The French no-claims coefficient only moves for claims where the rider's **liability is established**, in whole or in part. A non-fault accident, a theft, a fire or broken glass have no effect on it. The annual claims record only lists at-fault claims from the last five annual periods. The full mechanism is set out in the guide to the [motorcycle no-claims bonus system](/en/blog/motorcycle-insurance-bonus-malus/).

Conversely, failing to report a covered claim is a breach of contract. The insurer may cancel the policy if it establishes that the omission caused it prejudice, with a five-year entry in the AGIRA industry file, which makes finding a new insurer markedly harder.

## Key takeaways {#takeaways}

Reporting a motorcycle accident comes down to four markers. Five working days, reduced to two for a theft. A paper accident statement kept on the bike, because the official app stops working the moment someone is injured. Bodily injury reported however minor, or later symptoms will not be covered. And a delay that, when justified, is not enough to defeat the claim.
