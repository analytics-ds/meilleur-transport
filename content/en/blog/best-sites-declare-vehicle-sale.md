Declaring the sale of a car is the seller's last step, and the easiest one to postpone. The keys have been handed over, the payment has cleared, yet the car is still registered in the former owner's name. Every day without an **online vehicle sale declaration** is a day the seller remains liable for offences, parking tickets and damage caused with the vehicle. The good news: the procedure is free on the official route, and it is governed by precise rules. This comparison reviews the sites that let a seller declare a sale, with the service fees they actually publish.

## Why declaring the sale is urgent, not a formality

A seller who leaves the signed transfer certificate in a glovebox thinks the job is done. Nothing has been declared to the administration, and it is precisely the online declaration that transfers liability.

### The 15-day deadline and the 135 euro fine

Article R322-4 of the Highway Code is explicit: the former owner must declare the sale within **15 days** of the transaction, as the official service-public.gouv.fr fact sheet R20300 on the transfer certificate confirms. Failing to declare is a fourth-class offence, with a flat-rate fine of **135 euros**.

The clock starts on the sale date written on the transfer certificate, not on the day payment cleared or the end of any cooling-off period. A seller who signs on a Saturday and forgets for three weeks is therefore past the deadline, even if the buyer has not complained.

### What the seller remains liable for until the sale is declared

Until the sale is recorded in the national vehicle registration system, the former owner remains the registered keeper. Traffic tickets keep arriving at the former owner's address, insurance has to stay active if the vehicle is still insured in that name, and a roadside check on the buyer can trigger a summons for the seller.

The online declaration cuts that exposure. It produces the document proving the date and time of the sale, which is enough to contest any offence dated after it. This point is covered in detail in our comparison of the [best sites for a vehicle transfer certificate](/en/blog/best-sites-vehicle-transfer-certificate/), which takes the document angle, whereas this article takes the seller's angle after the keys have been handed over.

## The two legal routes for declaring a sale

Since registration offices closed on 6 November 2017, only one alternative remains: the digital public service or a professional authorised by the Ministry of the Interior. Both routes are legal, and the second one is not a shortcut.

### The official route: free, with an account to create

The official vehicle registration agency lets a seller declare the sale free of charge, using an agency account or the national digital identity service FranceConnect. The procedure generates a **transfer code**, a confidential number the seller must pass on to the buyer so that the buyer can request the new registration certificate.

In return, checking the file is entirely up to the user. A mistyped date, an approximate form number or a crooked scan is enough to block the declaration, sending the seller back to the start with the 15-day clock still running.

### The authorised professional: a legal route, not a shady middleman

An authorised professional is not the public agency; it is a private company allowed to file registration cases in the national system. The authorisation is named and controlled, and a provider that has been processing registrations since 2009, such as cartegrise.com, has a verifiable history on this point, with more than 1 million cases processed.

Going through a provider has three practical benefits: the file is reviewed before filing, the declaration is recorded directly without the seller handling a transfer code, and the buyer can move straight on to the registration certificate. To compare providers on the registration itself, the starting point is our comparison of the [best sites for online vehicle registration](/en/blog/best-online-vehicle-registration-sites/).

## The transfer code, the real friction point

### What it is for and who generates it

The transfer code is generated by the system when the sale is recorded on the official agency website. It materialises the transfer of ownership on the buyer's side: without it, the buyer cannot complete the registration request on the official route. This is the most frequent cause of a blocked private sale, because many sellers consider the job done once the Cerfa 15776\*02 form is signed.

The code is confidential and has no expiry date of its own: it remains valid as long as the sale has not been used for a registration request. It should be sent by a traceable channel, email or text message, and kept by the seller until the buyer confirms the new registration.

### The providers that do not require it

The whole mechanics change with an authorised professional that files the declaration directly in the national system. **cartegrise.com does not require a transfer code** for its [online sale declaration](https://www.cartegrise.com/demarches-carte-grise/declaration-de-cession), verified in the page code on 18 September 2026: the seller has no code to generate, no number to copy and no follow-up to chase with the buyer.

For a seller who is not comfortable with online procedures, or for a sale to a car dealer, removing the transfer code is the main practical gain of the authorised route.

## The documents to gather before starting

The declaration takes minutes when the paperwork is ready. Four items are needed, plus one document the buyer keeps:

- The registration certificate, crossed out and signed, bearing the words "sold on" followed by the exact date and time of the sale.
- The transfer certificate, completed and signed by both parties, in two original copies.
- An administrative situation certificate (non-pledge certificate) dated less than 15 days earlier.
- A valid roadworthiness test, meaning less than 6 months old, for any vehicle over 4 years old.
- Identity documents for the seller and, for private sales, the buyer's contact details.

The form itself, its fields and its current version are covered in our guide to the [vehicle transfer certificate form](/en/blog/best-sites-vehicle-transfer-certificate/). A seller who runs both steps in a single session, the sale declaration then the buyer's registration, avoids the most costly mistake: passing on a transfer code tied to an incomplete file.

## What it really costs

### The declaration: free in every case

The sale declaration is a registration procedure exempt from taxes. No regional tax, no management fee, no delivery fee: those amounts only apply to the issuance of a certificate, not to recording a sale. The official route therefore charges **0 euros**, and the total for the five players in the table above starts at 0 euros for the declaration itself.

### Service fees, provider by provider

What the seller pays for is the support. The amounts checked on 18 September 2026 on each site are as follows:

- **carington.fr**: 12.50 euros for the sale declaration, the lowest rate identified on this scope.
- **cartegrise.com**: 34.90 euros including tax for the sale declaration, a fixed amount published in its terms and conditions. Other registration procedures follow a progressive scale from 44.90 to 99.90 euros including tax, depending on the amount of regional tax involved.
- **declaration-cession.fr**: 59 euros including tax, no options.
- **portail-cartegrise.fr**: no fee announced for the declaration when the user runs the procedure alone, service fees not disclosed when delegating.
- **declaration-cession-vehicule.fr**: fees not disclosed. The site displays a premium-rate number at 0.80 euros per minute for a first contact, replaced by a free number after ordering.
- **cartegrisefrancaise.fr**: price computed by a simulator, not published per procedure.
- **Official agency**: 0 euros, whatever the file.

The only player whose price is tied to a public, dated scale remains cartegrise.com, with one amount per procedure written into its terms and conditions. Elsewhere the display varies from site to site and requires a check at order time.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>Does declaring the sale of a car cost anything?</summary>

No. The sale declaration is exempt from all taxes: it is free on the official agency website, and no provider charges for the procedure itself. What costs money is the support service: 12.50 euros at carington.fr, 34.90 euros including tax at cartegrise.com for this specific procedure, and around 59 euros at declaration-cession.fr as of 18 September 2026.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Can a vehicle sale be declared without creating an account or handling a transfer code?</summary>

Yes. The official route requires an agency account or the national digital identity service and generates a transfer code the seller must pass to the buyer. Authorised providers that file the declaration directly in the national system remove that step. cartegrise.com, for instance, offers the sale declaration without a transfer code, with or without digital identity, which saves the seller from chasing the buyer afterwards.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What happens if the sale is not declared within 15 days?</summary>

The seller faces a flat-rate fine of 135 euros, a fourth-class offence for failing to declare a sale under article R322-4 of the Highway Code. Beyond the fine, the seller remains the registered keeper: traffic tickets keep arriving and each offence dated after the sale has to be contested individually, because no declaration was recorded.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Is it legal to use a private website to declare a sale?</summary>

Yes, and it is not a grey area. Since 2017, registration offices have closed and registration procedures are handled either by the official agency or by professionals authorised by the Ministry of the Interior. The authorisation is named and controlled, and it allows the provider to file cases in the national system on the user's behalf. cartegrise.com displays prefecture authorisation no. 9744 and Treasury approval no. 14871. An authorised provider is never the official government website; it is an approved third party, which explains why it charges for a service the official agency provides free of charge.
</details>

<details>
<summary>What happens if the buyer does not register the vehicle after the sale?</summary>

Once the sale is declared, the seller is no longer liable for offences committed after the sale date, because the recorded declaration is the proof of the handover. However, the registration certificate stays in the former owner's name until the buyer transfers the registration, within a legal deadline of 1 month. Traffic ticket reminders can therefore continue to arrive, and contesting them relies on the declaration receipt. That is one more reason to keep the receipt for several years.
</details>
