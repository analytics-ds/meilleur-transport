The **electric scooter** is increasingly common in major French cities, driven by low-emission zones and a lower running cost compared with combustion models. Choosing the right power, range and budget for daily trips still matters. This guide covers the selection criteria, the 50cc and 125cc equivalents available in 2026, and the license and insurance requirements to know before buying.

## How to choose an electric scooter for the city

The first thing to decide is the **equivalence needed**: a 50cc-equivalent model, capped at 45 km/h, is enough for short trips in dense urban areas, while a 125cc-equivalent model allows riders to take faster urban roads and reach the suburbs without struggling in traffic.

**Real-world range** should always take priority over the range announced by the manufacturer, which is usually measured under optimal conditions. A daily round trip of 20 to 30 kilometers remains comfortable for almost every model on the market, but heavier use requires checking the real urban-cycle range, often 20 to 30% lower than the advertised figure.

**Weight and maneuverability** also matter, especially when parking or maneuvering in a tight two-wheeler parking spot. Removable batteries, available on several models, add real convenience: they avoid immobilizing the scooter for charging and let riders charge directly at the office or in an apartment, an advantage for anyone without a parking spot with an outlet.

Finally, the **budget** should include the purchase price as well as maintenance costs, notably lower than a combustion scooter thanks to the absence of oil changes and reduced wear on mechanical parts.

**Trip frequency** also shapes the right battery capacity. Daily use with a single overnight charge is enough for most urban riders, but someone making several round trips a day will benefit from a removable-battery or dual-battery model, to recharge without waiting hours between trips.

## Best 50cc-equivalent electric scooters for the city

The 50cc-equivalent segment targets strictly urban trips and young riders holding an AM license. The **Yadea G5** is one of the market references, with a top speed of 45 km/h and an announced range of up to 55 kilometers, enough for short daily use.

The **Peugeot e-Kisbee** bets on a compact format and simple handling, with a range of up to 110 kilometers thanks to a dual-battery setup. This model suits riders alternating between commuting and occasional longer trips without wanting to move up a vehicle category.

This segment remains the most affordable, with prices generally between 1,500 and 2,500 euros depending on battery capacity and equipment. It mainly suits riders who don't plan on suburban or fast-road trips.

Other models such as the **Vmoto Citi** or the **Easy-Watts E-Presto** round out this entry-level range, with similar specs: around 45 km/h top speed and an announced range of roughly 80 to 100 kilometers. These scooters mainly target a first electric purchase or secondary household use, alongside a car or a combustion scooter kept for longer trips.

## Best 125cc-equivalent electric scooters for the city

For trips combining city and suburban roads, the 125cc equivalent offers better-suited speed and range. The **Silence S01** has become a European best-seller, with an announced range of 130 kilometers and performance close to a combustion 125cc.

The **Horwin SK3 Plus** and the **Segway E300SE** round out this segment with a similar range, around 130 kilometers, and top speeds between 100 and 105 km/h, more than enough for urban and suburban traffic.

This segment suits riders covering around thirty kilometers a day or more, or who want a comfortable range buffer without recharging every night. Prices generally range from 4,000 to 6,000 euros depending on the brand and battery capacity.

Some higher-end models, such as the **Speed Rider SR1** fitted with two removable batteries, exceed 200 kilometers of range and rival combustion 125cc scooters for suburban use, at a higher budget. These versions mainly suit riders combining a long commute with regular business trips.

## Running costs and maintenance of an electric scooter in the city

The main economic advantage of the electric scooter lies in its **lower running cost**. Without a combustion engine, there is no oil change, no spark plugs, and no air filter to replace, which significantly lightens maintenance bills over the ownership period. The main maintenance items are limited to tires, brake pads, and, longer term, battery replacement.

The **cost per kilometer in electricity** stays well below that of fuel, with a full charge generally costing a few tens of cents depending on the electricity rate and battery capacity. Over a daily use of several thousand kilometers a year, the gap with an equivalent combustion scooter quickly becomes significant.

Some local authorities and the French state also offer an **ecological bonus** or a conversion incentive for buying an electric two-wheeler, which reduces the effective purchase price compared with the catalog prices mentioned above. Conditions and amounts vary depending on household income and place of residence, so it's worth checking eligibility before buying.

## License, insurance and charging: what to know before buying

The **AM license** (formerly BSR) is enough for a 50cc equivalent, while a 125cc equivalent requires an A1 license, an A2 license, or a 7-hour training course for car license holders of more than two years. This training, mandatory since 2011, applies the same way to combustion and electric versions of the 125cc segment.

**Insurance** remains mandatory regardless of the scooter's power, with a minimum third-party liability policy. Premiums vary depending on the rider's profile and the two-wheeler's equipment; our comparison of the [best scooter insurers](/en/blog/best-scooter-insurers/) covers the offers best suited to this type of vehicle.

On the **charging** side, most models need between 3 and 8 hours for a full charge on a standard household outlet. Removable batteries, found on a growing share of models, make daily charging easier by avoiding immobilizing the scooter at an outdoor outlet.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>Do you need a license to ride an electric scooter in the city?</summary>

An electric scooter equivalent to 50cc, capped at 45 km/h, can be ridden with an **AM license** (formerly BSR), available from age 14, or with no formality for people born before 1988. A 125cc-equivalent model requires an A1 license, an A2 license, or a car license completed with a 7-hour training course for drivers who have held it for more than two years.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What insurance is mandatory for an electric scooter?</summary>

Like any motorized vehicle on public roads, an electric scooter must be insured at least **for third-party liability**, regardless of its power. This coverage protects against damage caused to others. Premiums generally range from 150 to 250 euros a year depending on the scooter's power and the rider's profile.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What range can you expect from an electric scooter in real urban use?</summary>

In real urban traffic (frequent stops, acceleration, weather), the actual **range** is most often between 50 and 120 kilometers depending on the model, below the figures announced under constant-speed test cycles. 50cc-equivalent models typically cap around 55 to 100 km, while 125cc-equivalent models regularly exceed 130 km.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How long does it take to charge an electric scooter?</summary>

Most models need between **3 and 8 hours** for a full charge on a standard household outlet. Scooters with removable batteries make charging easier, since they can be charged at the office or at home without immobilizing the vehicle.

</details>
