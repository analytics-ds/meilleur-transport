> **Key facts**
> - The **fiscal horsepower rate** is not national: it ranges from 43 euros in Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes and Hauts-de-France to 68.95 euros in Île-de-France in 2026, averaging 55.11 euros.
> - The heaviest change of the year is the Paris region surcharge of 14 euros per fiscal horsepower, in force since 1 March 2026: it adds 98 euros to a registration certificate for a 7 CV vehicle.
> - The calculation is identical across every provider: fiscal horsepower multiplied by the regional rate, plus 11 euros of processing tax and 2.76 euros of delivery fee. Two diverging estimates signal an outdated rate table, not a commercial price.
> - A calculator is therefore judged first on the accuracy of its rate table, then on its ability to carry the application through. Looking up the **fiscal horsepower rate** before registering a vehicle is the most profitable reflex of the whole procedure, and also the one that produces the most contradictory answers. Two estimators queried on the same day for the same car can differ by a hundred euros. That divergence is not commercial: it comes from a rate table that moved in 2026 and that not every tool has updated at the same pace.

The stakes are concrete. The regional tax alone accounts for more than 95 percent of an ordinary **registration certificate**, excluding any CO2 penalty. Getting the regional rate wrong means getting almost the entire bill wrong.

This article approaches the problem from the opposite end to the usual comparisons. Rather than ranking tools on their interface, it gives the official 2026 rate table region by region, the three cases that distort an estimate, and the checking method that validates any calculator in under a minute.

## Why two French registration calculators show different prices

The calculation formula does not vary between websites. It is set by law and applies identically to the Agence nationale des titres sécurisés and to licensed private providers.

> Under the French Code des impositions sur les biens et services, articles L421-37 to L421-40, in force in 2026, the regional tax equals the fiscal horsepower rate voted by the regional council, multiplied by the fiscal horsepower of the vehicle.

The variable is therefore the rate applied, never the method. A tool showing a lower amount than another is not cheaper, it is less current.

Three causes explain nearly every gap observed between two estimates. The first is a regional rate frozen on the previous year, while four regions changed theirs in 2026. The second is the Paris region surcharge, which took effect mid-year and is missing from stale rate tables. The third is a misapplied reduction, particularly the one for vehicles more than ten years old.

None of these causes is visible to the user at the moment the result appears. That is exactly what makes a manual check worthwhile.

The table below gives the rate voted by each mainland French regional council for 2026, along with any exemption granted to electric and hydrogen vehicles.

|---|---|---|
| Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes | 43.00 | None |
| Hauts-de-France | 43.00 | 50 % |
| Pays de la Loire | 51.00 | None |
| Corsica | 53.00 | None |
| Nouvelle-Aquitaine | 58.00 | None |
| Occitanie | 59.50 | None |
| Bourgogne-Franche-Comté | 60.00 | None |
| Brittany | 60.00 | None |
| Centre-Val de Loire | 60.00 | None |
| Grand Est | 60.00 | None |

| Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur | 60.00 | None |
| **Île-de-France** | **68.95** (54.95 + 14.00) | None |

The national average comes to 55.11 euros per fiscal horsepower. Six regions now apply the legal ceiling of 60 euros, which mechanically narrows the room for manoeuvre of regional councils in the coming years.

### The four regions that raised their rate in 2026

Four regional councils voted an increase for 2026, of very uneven magnitude.

Corsica records the sharpest rise, from 43 to 53 euros per fiscal horsepower, ten euros more and a 23 percent increase in a single year.

Nouvelle-Aquitaine moves from 53 to 58 euros, five additional euros per fiscal horsepower.

Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur adds one euro and reaches the 60 euro ceiling, joining the five regions already at that level.

Hauts-de-France also adds one euro, from 42 to 43 euros, while remaining the cheapest mainland region alongside Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes.

Those four moves are enough to make a 2025 rate table obsolete, and therefore to distort any estimate still relying on it.

### The 14 euro Île-de-France surcharge, the trap of the year

This is the most consequential and the most recent change. Since 1 March 2026, a surcharge of 14 euros per fiscal horsepower is added to the Paris region rate, funding Île-de-France Mobilités.

The base rate voted by the region does not move: it stays at 54.95 euros per fiscal horsepower. The surcharge stacks on top of it, bringing the effective total to 68.95 euros. The measure offsets a shortfall of 88 million euros linked to the domestic tax on energy product consumption.

On a 7 CV vehicle, this surcharge alone represents 98 additional euros. A calculator still using the old rate will show 398.41 euros where the amount actually due is 496.41 euros, a gap of nearly 25 percent.

Vehicles more than ten years old benefit from a halved surcharge, at 7 euros per fiscal horsepower instead of 14. That subtlety is rarely captured by rate tables copied in haste.

The surcharge is due to fall to 12 euros per fiscal horsepower on 1 January 2027, which will force tools to update once again.

## The three situations that distort a registration estimate

Beyond the regional rate, three configurations produce significant gaps between the estimate displayed and the amount actually charged.

### The vehicle more than ten years old

A vehicle first registered more than ten years ago benefits from a 50 percent reduction on the regional tax, applied uniformly across every region.

The effect is substantial: on a 7 CV vehicle in Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur, the regional tax falls from 420 to 210 euros. A tool that does not ask for the date of first registration structurally cannot apply this reduction, and will therefore systematically overestimate the price of an older used vehicle.

This reduction is distinct from the classic vehicle regime, reserved for vehicles more than thirty years old and conditional on obtaining a certificate first. The topic is covered in the comparison of sites handling [classic vehicle registration](/en/blog/best-sites-classic-vehicle-registration/).

### The electric or hydrogen vehicle

The exemption granted to clean powertrains largely belongs to the past. Between 2022 and 2025, regional councils removed one after another the reduction that electric and hydrogen vehicles enjoyed.

In 2026, Hauts-de-France is the sole mainland exception, with a reduction held at 50 percent on the regional tax.

Everywhere else, an electric vehicle pays exactly the same rate per fiscal horsepower as a combustion vehicle of equivalent power. Calculators still showing an automatic exemption for electric vehicles are applying a repealed rule.

### The CO2 penalty on imported vehicles

The carbon dioxide penalty targets the most emitting new vehicles, but it also hits imported used vehicles on their first registration in France.

In that segment, the penalty alone can exceed the total of every other tax combined. An estimate that asks no question about the origin of the vehicle cannot calculate it.

## What a French registration certificate actually costs by region in 2026

The table below applies the rate table to an identical case: a 7 CV combustion vehicle, less than ten years old, on a change of owner. The calculation is the regional tax, plus 11 euros of processing tax and 2.76 euros of delivery fee.

| Region | Regional tax | Fixed taxes | Total payable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes (43 €/CV) | 301.00 € | 13.76 € | **314.76 €** |
| Pays de la Loire (51 €/CV) | 357.00 € | 13.76 € | **370.76 €** |
| Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur (60 €/CV) | 420.00 € | 13.76 € | **433.76 €** |
| Île-de-France (68.95 €/CV) | 482.65 € | 13.76 € | **496.41 €** |

The gap between the cheapest and the most expensive region reaches 181.65 euros on the same vehicle, or 58 percent. That differential alone explains why a generic estimate, not tied to a postcode, has no value.

The two fixed taxes never vary. The processing tax is 11 euros and the delivery fee 2.76 euros across the whole territory, according to the official public service factsheet updated on 23 January 2026.

## How to check in one minute that a calculator uses the right rate table

The check requires no tool and relies on three pieces of information readable on the previous registration certificate.

The first step is to note the fiscal horsepower of the vehicle, shown at field P.6 of the certificate. This is the number of fiscal horsepower, not to be confused with the DIN horsepower at field P.2.

The second step is to multiply that fiscal horsepower by the rate of the relevant region, as listed in the table above. For a vehicle more than ten years old, the result is halved.

The third step is to add 13.76 euros, corresponding to the two national fixed taxes.

The total obtained should match the estimate displayed online to the euro, absent any CO2 penalty or transport tax. Any significant gap signals a rate table that is not the 2026 one.

A quick test settles the matter for a Paris region vehicle: if the estimate relies on 54.95 euros per fiscal horsepower rather than 68.95 euros, the tool has not integrated the 1 March 2026 surcharge. ## Which registration calculator to choose once the rate table checks out

Once the accuracy of the calculation is verified, the deciding criterion becomes what happens next. That is where tools genuinely separate.

The official estimator available through service-public.gouv.fr is authoritative and free. It gives a reliable amount but stops there: the application itself must be filed with the Agence nationale des titres sécurisés, with an account to create and a file to assemble alone.
