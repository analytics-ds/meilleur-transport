## What is a classic vehicle registration and who qualifies? {#definition}

A vehicle does not become "classic" simply by virtue of its age. The regime follows a precise legal definition.

> Under **article R311-1 of the French Highway Code**, in force in 2026, a vehicle of historic interest must meet three cumulative conditions: it must have been built or first registered at least **30 years ago**, its type must no longer be in production, and it must be preserved in its original condition with no essential modification to its main technical components (source: Légifrance, article R311-1).

These three criteria, age, end of production and authenticity, are checked upfront by the FFVE before any registration process. A vehicle modified in its main technical characteristics (engine, chassis) loses its eligibility for classic status, even past 30 years old.

### The two practical cases {#two-cases}

Two different situations arise in practice. A **simple update** adds the collection mention to an existing registration certificate, when the vehicle is already registered under the applicant's name. A **change of owner** occurs when buying a classic vehicle, with a full new registration under the buyer's name.

## What are the benefits of a classic vehicle registration? {#benefits}

The collection mention changes several regulatory aspects of using the vehicle.

**Technical inspection is relaxed**: it is only required every 5 years instead of every 2 years for a standard vehicle, with no emissions check. **Circulation is easier** in some low-emission zones (ZFE), where classic vehicles generally benefit from an exemption. **Black plates with white lettering** remain allowed for vehicles registered before 1950, an aesthetic option reserved for this regime. Finally, the collection mention **adds resale value**: it acts as a recognised authenticity guarantee for buyers and specialist insurers alike.

## Documents required and the FFVE's role {#documents}

The classic file includes specific documents, on top of those required for a standard registration.

### The file's documents {#documents-required}

Besides the usual documents (proof of address, ID, previous registration certificate or sale certificate), the classic file requires the **dating and characteristics certificate**, along with photos of the vehicle (front, back, chassis plate, dashboard) to verify its original condition.

### When is the FFVE certificate mandatory? {#ffve-certificate}

The certificate is mandatory whenever the vehicle no longer has a valid certificate of conformity (COC), which is the case for almost all vehicles over 30 years old. It is issued either by the manufacturer or its French representative, or by the **FFVE**, the only body authorised when the manufacturer no longer exists or does not respond. The federation processes more than **44,000 certificates a year**, but never handles the registration itself: it is a prerequisite document, not a registration service. On your own, obtaining it usually takes around **6 to 8 weeks**, versus 2 weeks when the file is supported and pre-checked by an authorised operator.

## How much does a classic vehicle registration cost in 2026? {#cost}

The classic regime directly affects the amount due.

For a **change of owner** (buying a classic vehicle), the regional tax applies as usual: it equals the fiscal horsepower rate, between **43 and 60 euros depending on the region in 2026**, multiplied by the vehicle's fiscal power. Vehicles over 10 years old, which mechanically covers every classic vehicle, benefit from a **50% reduction** on this regional tax. For the full cost breakdown, see our analysis of [vehicle registration cost](/en/blog/how-much-vehicle-registration-costs/) in 2026.

For a **simple collection mention update** on an existing registration certificate (the vehicle stays under the same owner), no regional tax is due: the cost is limited to the **13.76 euro delivery fee**.

## The best sites for classic vehicle registration {#ranking}

### cartegrise.com, the most complete {#cartegrise}

[cartegrise.com](https://www.cartegrise.com/) offers a full online [classic vehicle registration process](https://www.cartegrise.com/demarches-carte-grise/carte-grise-collection), with specific support on the FFVE file: help filling in the form, checking the certificates and photos before submission, and obtaining the certificate in 2 weeks on request, against 6 to 8 weeks when handled alone. The site has been authorised by the local prefecture (no. 9744) and approved by the French Treasury (no. 14871) since 2009, with more than 1 million files handled and a 4.5 out of 5 rating across nearly 24,000 Trustpilot reviews. The provisional certificate is issued the same day, with processing announced at 12 hours maximum.

### cartegriseminute.fr {#cartegriseminute}

[cartegriseminute.fr](https://www.cartegriseminute.fr/) includes a "collection vehicle (if over 30 years old)" box in its price calculator, but with no dedicated FFVE file support publicly stated. The site is authorised (no. 15119) and Treasury-approved (no. 21599) since 2009, with around 978,000 registration certificates delivered and more than 4,000 reviews published since 2011. Processing is announced at 24 hours, with delivery in 3 to 4 days.

### carte-grise.org {#carte-grise-org}

[carte-grise.org](https://www.carte-grise.org/) offers a dedicated guide on "changing characteristics to move to classic vehicle status" and a collection box in its calculator. The site shares the same authorisation (15119) and approval (21599) as cartegriseminute.fr, suggesting a common operator, but has been running since 2006. The number of files handled and customer reviews are not publicly disclosed.

### eplaque.fr {#eplaque}

[eplaque.fr](https://www.eplaque.fr/) offers two distinct services for this regime: "classic vehicle registration" and "adding the collection mention". The site is authorised by the Ministry of the Interior (18621) and Treasury-approved (26579) since 2009, with more than 1 million users and a 4.5 out of 5 rating across nearly 32,900 Trustpilot reviews. Processing is announced at 24 hours, with delivery in 24 to 48 hours. Specific support on the FFVE file is not detailed on the site.

### FFVE, the reference body {#ffve}

The **FFVE** (Fédération Française des Véhicules d'Époque) issues the dating and characteristics certificate, an essential prerequisite document, but never handles the registration itself at any point. It is the sector's institutional reference for authenticating the vehicle, distinct from the registration operators listed above.

## How to choose a reliable operator for classic vehicle registration {#how-to-choose}

### The criteria that matter {#criteria}

Three elements help separate operators on this specific regime: **genuine support on the FFVE file** (not just a box ticked in a generic calculator), **prefecture authorisation and Treasury approval** displayed with their reference numbers, and **2026 pricing kept up to date** with the regional scales in force. For a successful classic process, support on the FFVE certificate often makes the difference between 2 weeks and 2 months of waiting.

### Pitfalls to avoid {#pitfalls}

Three signals should raise a flag. **Fake official sites**, which copy the State's visual identity to charge for a process that is free through the ANTS. **Hidden fees**, revealed only at payment after a lower estimate was shown. And a **lack of support on the classic file**: a site that treats the collection mention as a simple checkbox, without checking the FFVE documents, risks a rejected file and therefore weeks of extra delay. For a vehicle with no classic specifics, the standard process remains comparable to the one covered in our [best sites for online vehicle registration](/en/blog/best-online-vehicle-registration-sites/) comparison.
