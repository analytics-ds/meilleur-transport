## In short

1. The only compulsory guarantee, third-party liability, **covers neither the rider, nor the bike, nor the gear**. It protects everybody except you.
2. The guarantee most often missing from a policy is also the most consequential: **rider injury cover**. A rider responsible for his own fall is compensated by nobody without it.
3. Differences between insurers are not in the policy names, which all look alike, but in **the limits and the exclusions**: from 800 to 2,000 euros on riding gear, with or without an excess, with or without assistance starting at your doorstep.

The usual reflex is to compare three quotes on price and sign the cheapest. The problem is that two policies priced the same can cover very different things, and the missing line only shows up on the day of the claim. This guide goes through each guarantee, then compares the main motorcycle insurers on those exact points.

![Rider checking an insurance policy](/images/meilleur-assureur-moto-reputation/meilleur-assureur-moto-reputation-2.webp "Essential motorcycle insurance cover")

## The five guarantees that matter, and what each one pays for {#table}

| Guarantee | What it covers | What it does not cover | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Third-party liability | Damage caused to others, bodily and material | The rider, his bike, his gear | Compulsory |
| Rider injury cover | The rider's bodily injuries, even at fault, even alone | Material damage | Optional, decisive |
| Theft and fire | Theft, attempted theft, fire | Damage without theft | Optional |
| Riding gear | Helmet, gloves, jacket, sometimes the airbag vest | Anything above the limit, depreciation applied | Optional or included |
| Comprehensive damage | Damage to the bike, including a solo fall | Policy exclusions, excess deducted | Optional |

The second line is the one to remember. Every other guarantee deals with hardware, which can be repaired or replaced. That one deals with the rider, and it is the only one whose absence can turn into a lasting loss of income.

## Third-party liability never protects the rider {#liability}

It is the legal floor: every registered motor vehicle must be covered, even one that never moves. It compensates the victim of a crash caused by the rider, with no ceiling on bodily injury in most policies.

Its limit is structural: **it is built for other people**. A rider who causes a crash sees the third parties compensated and receives nothing, neither for his machine nor for his injuries. The same logic applies to a solo fall with no third party involved, which accounts for a significant share of two-wheeler accidents.

That is why a bare third-party policy is rarely a good calculation, even on a low-value bike. The economics are not about the machine, they are about the rider.

## Rider injury cover, the real blind spot {#rider-cover}

It steps in where liability stops: medical costs not reimbursed by the health system, lost income during time off, adapting a home or a vehicle after lasting consequences, and a lump sum paid to relatives in the event of death.

Three things to check before signing, because they differ wildly between policies:

- **The trigger threshold.** Most guarantees only activate above a certain level of permanent impairment. Below it, nothing is paid.
- **The amount.** It is expressed as a maximum lump sum, rarely reached, and scaled to the level of impairment assessed by the expert.
- **The exclusions.** Track days, riding a machine outside the licence held, or carrying a passenger outside the policy conditions.

At AMV this takes the form of the **Individuelle Pilote** option: it pays a lump sum, up to 800,000 euros, to the policyholder in the event of permanent functional impairment, or to his beneficiaries in the event of death, following a crash even when he is at fault. Whether you ride a roadster, an adventure bike or a maxi-scooter, this guarantee is worth looking at: it is the only line of the policy that deals with the rider rather than the machine.

## Riding gear, the line everyone underestimates {#gear}

A full set of approved gear, helmet, jacket, gloves, trousers and boots, comfortably exceeds 1,000 euros, and can be destroyed in a fall that causes no injury at all. It is also the guarantee whose limits vary the most between insurers.

| Insurer | Gear limit | Availability |
|---|---|---|
| **AMV** | **2,000 €** | Included from the theft and fire level |
| Mutuelle des Motards | 1,600 € | Included or optional |

What matters is not the limit alone but **depreciation**. Most policies apply a reduction based on the age of the gear, which can halve the payout on a three-year-old helmet. Some policies provide replacement at purchase value for a limited period after buying: that is the most favourable clause, and it is worth checking. ## Assistance and replacement bike: read the detail, not the label {#assistance}

Two policies both advertising "assistance included" can behave in opposite ways. Three things to look at:

- **The trigger distance.** Some policies only respond beyond a set distance from home. A breakdown at your own front door is then on you, and that is one of the most common cases.
- **The replacement bike.** Rarely included by default, yet it is what makes the difference when the motorcycle is the main vehicle and stays off the road for weeks.
- **The scope.** Assistance operates on made-up roads. It does not follow the machine off-road, whatever the bike is built for.

At AMV, assistance is offered as an option across the whole range, with an intervention possible even right outside your home, whether it is a crash, a theft, a puncture or a breakdown. The vehicle is recovered or towed, the rider and passengers are taken home.

## Choosing according to your profile {#profile}

Three cases cover most situations.

**A low-value bike, an experienced rider.** Priority goes to rider injury cover and gear, not to damage cover. Compensating a machine worth 2,000 euros matters little against the real cost of a bodily injury.

**A recent machine, daily use.** Collision damage, then comprehensive damage, become relevant, particularly for the solo fall that no other guarantee covers. The full policy could be justified as long as the value of the bike stays high.

**A new licence.** Pricing is based on the model, the engine capacity and the rider profile. A two-wheeler specialist knows how to credit previous 125cc experience where many generalist grids ignore it, and that weighs more on the bill than the choice of policy level itself. ## The takeaway {#takeaway}

The right question is not "third party or comprehensive", it is "what is compensated by nobody if I do not buy it". Two lines answer that: rider injury cover and gear cover. Those are the ones to look at first, before comparing prices, because they are the only ones you cannot fix after the claim.
